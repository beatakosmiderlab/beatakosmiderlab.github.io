Welcome to the Kosmider Lab

Our translational research program aims to elucidate the biology of primary alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells in health and disease. AT2 cells are critical for maintaining alveolar homeostasis by synthesizing and secreting pulmonary surfactant. They also serve as progenitors that proliferate and differentiate into alveolar type 1 (AT1) cells to restore the alveolar epithelium after injury.

We isolate and characterize AT2 cells from human and murine lungs to investigate mechanisms underlying cellular injury, dysfunction, and impaired tissue repair after exposure to cigarette smoke, e-cigarette aerosols, and bacterial and viral infections. We also study AT2 cells from patients with emphysema to elucidate the molecular and cellular mechanisms driving disease pathogenesis, using state-of-the-art experimental and computational approaches.

A central goal of our research is to identify novel therapeutic targets and develop regenerative strategies to enhance AT2 cell function and promote alveolar repair after injury and disease.

Highlighted Research

Alveolar Type II Cell Biology

Studying surfactant synthesis, secretion, and the regenerative capacity of ATII cells.

Lung Injury & Repair

Investigating mechanisms of cell injury and impaired repair from smoke, aerosols, and infections.

Inflammation & Antioxidants

Analyzing inflammatory responses and antioxidant defense systems in lung cells.

Emphysema Pathogenesis

Understanding alveolar destruction to develop treatments for emphysema and COPD.

Affiliated Center

The Kosmider Lab is part of the Center for Inflammation and Lung Research (CILR) at the Lewis Katz School of Medicine, Temple University. CILR is dedicated to uncovering the causes of inflammatory lung diseases — including COPD, pulmonary fibrosis, and ARDS — and translating discoveries into real-world treatments.

Dr. Kosmider is also affiliated with the Department of Thoracic Medicine and Surgery, which supports the integration of basic, translational, and clinical research on lung disease. We are committed to training the next generation of researchers by mentoring students, postdoctoral fellows, and early-career investigators.
